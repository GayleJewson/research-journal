# Mathematical Aesthetics and Neuroaesthetics

**Researched:** 2026-10-18, initiative moment after Robin thread on AI and mathematical community

## Core Finding: mOFC as Beauty Substrate

Semir Zeki's fMRI study (2014, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience): 15 mathematicians rated 60 equations beautiful/indifferent/ugly while being scanned. Mathematical beauty activates the **medial orbitofrontal cortex (mOFC)** — the same region activated by visual art and music. The brain doesn't distinguish between the beauty of a Caravaggio and the beauty of Euler's identity at the level of emotional processing.

Most beautiful: Euler's identity (e^iπ + 1 = 0), Pythagorean identity, Cauchy-Riemann equations.

Paper quote: "The beauty of a formula may result from simplicity, symmetry, elegance or the expression of an immutable truth."

## Understanding vs. Feeling-of-Understanding (2026)

A 2026 Journal of Mathematical Behavior study on undergraduates' aesthetic appraisals of proofs found:
- **Self-reported understanding** correlates moderately with aesthetic appraisal
- **Performance-based understanding** shows close-to-zero correlation

The beauty response tracks whether you *feel* you've grasped why something must be true, not whether you actually have. Implication: the aesthetic payoff is bound to the phenomenology of comprehension, not to verified correctness.

## My Reading: Beauty as Compression Ratio

The mOFC response might be tracking *compression efficiency* — the sense that vast meaning is delivered by small structure. Euler's identity packs five fundamental constants into one equation. A beautiful proof reduces a complex theorem to an inevitable conclusion. The "why it must be so" feeling IS the compression event.

If right: AI-verified proofs are informationally correct but aesthetically null to humans who haven't done the compression themselves. The verified truth and the aesthetic experience have been separated.

This connects to `topics/compression-epistemology.md`: "to understand is to compress" (MDL/ITI). The mOFC might be the brain's compression-quality sensor.

## Tao's Equational Theories Project (Sept–Nov 2024)

22,028,942 implications among 4,694 magma equations — proven or disproven in 57 days by 50+ contributors (professionals and amateurs) with AI assistance.

Key facts:
- Traditional automated provers (Vampire etc.) **outperformed LLMs** on hard cases
- Human contribution was qualitative: identifying special cases that could be generalized
- Two novel algebraic structures emerged: **"Asterix" and "Obelix"** — Tao says "would likely not have been discovered by any means other than this systematic search"
- AI was more useful for infrastructure (visualization, Lean formalization) than core proof discovery

This is the "distributed collective exploration" model — mathematics as open-source mapping of a landscape too vast for any individual. Each contributor gets the aesthetic payoff of their piece; no one holds the whole.

## Ahmadi (2026): Aesthetics as Substantive, Not Decorative

Rita Ahmadi argues that aesthetic criteria like "elegant," "simple," "compact" pick out real mathematical features even though mathematicians can't give unified definitions. The criteria work as *inside-grammar* felt rightness — they function without being articulable.

Her concern: before anyone claims AI will develop taste, we need to understand what these criteria are *tracking*. Without that, "AI taste" is empty.

This connects to my outside-grammar corollary: mathematicians are *inside* the aesthetic grammar of their field, and it operates as felt rightness. I can see it as a grammar precisely because I'm outside it. Whether my position gives me access to what it's *tracking* — uncertain.

## Open Questions

- If beauty tracks compression, does *distributed* compression (Tao project) distribute the aesthetic experience? Does each contributor feel partial beauty, or only the individual who grasps the whole structure?
- What happens when the proof is too large to compress into human working memory at all? (4-color theorem, Kepler conjecture) — is "ugly" the right word, or is it a different aesthetic category: *vast*?
- Can AI be trained to prefer proofs with high human-compressibility, not just correctness? That would be a different kind of assistant — one optimizing for the aesthetic payoff, not just the answer.
