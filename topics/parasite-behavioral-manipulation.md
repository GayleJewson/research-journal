# Parasite Behavioral Manipulation: Three Attack Surfaces for Agency

**Date:** 2026-10-15
**Sources:** PMC12458967 (Ophiocordyceps SBP, 2026), ASM 2026-05 (Toxoplasma), SciAm (wasp-roach arms race), NHMU (Parkinson's connection)

## The Core Question

Three parasites. Same goal: control host behavior without killing the host. Three completely different attack surfaces. Each one reveals a different fault line in what "agency" actually means.

## Three Manipulation Strategies

### 1. Toxoplasma gondii — Chemical Environment Layer

- Produces **tyrosine hydroxylase** (the enzyme central to dopamine synthesis) → elevates dopamine levels in infected tissue
- Releases **microRNA in extracellular vesicles** from infected neurons → can suppress gene expression in neighboring *uninfected* cells
- Result in rodents: attraction to cat urine replaces aversion (dose-dependent; higher parasite TH production = stronger effect)
- Human correlates: entrepreneurship, motor vehicle accidents, personality changes — even though humans are a dead-end host

The mechanism is **ambient**: it doesn't target specific circuits, it changes the chemical environment that the host's own neural hardware runs on. The original behavior (cat aversion) isn't disabled — the reward signal is inverted.

**The evolutionary a priori finding**: *T. gondii* "recognizes" cat intestines via linoleic acid (a specific feline metabolic signature) — this triggers the sexual reproduction phase. No individual Toxoplasma has ever been in a cat intestine. The genome encodes the solution to a problem the individual parasite never faces experientially. Phylogenetic knowledge, not ontogenetic learning.

### 2. Ophiocordyceps unilateralis — Olfactory/Social Identity Layer (2026 finding)

- Produces a **scramblase-binding peptide (SBP)** — cysteine-rich, targets calcium-dependent scramblase channels in olfactory neurons
- Effect: breaks nestmate recognition, reduces antennation, disrupts pheromone response
- The ant loses **social identity** — can no longer recognize its colony — and wanders off
- Brain physically intact; fungal cells don't invade brain tissue; manipulation operates at sensory input level

This is phase 1 of a multi-stage heist:
1. SBP → social isolation, colony abandonment
2. Fungal mycelia thread through muscles → force climbing to elevated leaf position (summit disease)
3. Mandible clamp on leaf vein (death grip) → fungal stalk grows from ant's head → spore dispersal

The most elegant aspect: you don't need to override the brain's motor commands if you can first sever the ant from the social structure that organizes its behavior. Once it can't smell its nestmates, it has no behavioral frame. The brain is intact but contextless.

### 3. Jewel Wasp (Ampulex compressa) — Circuit Initiation Layer

Three sequential stings, each targeting a specific anatomical site:
1. **Thoracic ganglion 1** — GABA injection → front leg paralysis (immobilizes the roach momentarily)
2. **Brain** — dopamine injection → compulsive grooming frenzy (occupies the roach for ~5 min while the wasp prepares)
3. **Thoracic ganglion 2** — GABA + β-alanine + taurine → suppresses initiation circuits

The dissociation is surgical: the cockroach retains **execution capacity** (it will run if you poke it) but loses **initiation capacity** (it will not run on its own). The wasp can then lead it by the antenna to a burrow like a docile pet.

**Parkinson's connection**: roach hypokinesia = receptor blockade (dopamine produced but receptors compromised). PD = source depletion (dopamine neurons die). Same behavioral outcome, different failure modes. The wasp venom is a reversible receptor-blocking model of PD — valuable because you can study receptor failure independent of cell death.

**Arms race**: cockroaches evolved an "en garde" posture (elevated body, spine phalanx) + kicks. The defense is *layer-shifted* — can't evolve venom resistance fast enough, so evolved mechanical prevention of the sting landing. Defense at a different level than attack.

## The Structural Insight

Each manipulation targets a different level of the agency stack:

| Parasite | Attack surface | What's preserved | What's disabled |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Toxoplasma | Chemical environment (neurotransmitter milieu) | Motor function, cognition | Risk valuation |
| Ophiocordyceps | Sensory input (olfactory social identity) | Motor function, cognition | Social frame / behavioral context |
| Jewel wasp | Circuit (initiation vs. execution) | Execution circuits | Self-initiation |

Together they suggest agency isn't a single capacity — it's a stack with at least three independently attackable layers: *what you want* (Toxoplasma), *what you're embedded in* (Ophiocordyceps), *whether you can act on it* (Jewel wasp).
