# Herculaneum Papyri: The Vesuvius Challenge Discoveries

**First explored:** 2026-10-19

## What Is This

The Villa of the Papyri at Herculaneum — belonging to Lucius Calpurnius Piso Caesonius, Julius Caesar's father-in-law — contained the only substantial library to survive from classical antiquity: ~1,800 scrolls, carbonized in the eruption of Vesuvius in 79 CE. Physical unrolling destroys them. Virtual unrolling (phase-contrast X-ray micro-tomography + ML ink detection) doesn't.

The Vesuvius Challenge (launched 2023) has been crowdsourcing the decoding. In June 2026, they read **PHerc. 1667** — an entire scroll — end to end for the first time.

## What PHerc. 1667 Contains

A **Stoic philosophical treatise on ethics**, 2nd century BC, ~22 surviving columns across ~1.4 meters. Identified as Stoic by content and by the final preserved column, which names **Aristocreon** — nephew and disciple of Chrysippus, the third head of the Stoic school.

Key fragments:
- "we will inquire into something, but we will not grasp it, if in some way we depart from ourselves and from our own nature"
- "Having strained ourselves to the utmost through research and learning... possessing the same practical wisdom"
- Discussion of *hormē* (impulse) — the Stoic theory of rational agency: how thought moves to action
- On goods and evils: "from the opposite evils there will be neither anything good — let alone beautiful — nor anything bad"

This is surprising. The collection is predominantly **Epicurean** (mostly Philodemus). Finding a Stoic text suggests greater philosophical diversity in the villa's library than assumed. These were rival schools; Piso collected both.

## Other Recent Discoveries

- **PHerc. 172**: On Vices by Philodemus — 70+ columns on negative character traits (greed, arrogance, flattery). Explicitly Epicurean.
- **On Gods, Book 8** by Philodemus — proving his work extended well beyond the previously known Book 1.
- A scroll with early details about **Zeno of Citium**, founder of Stoicism (dates unclear).

## The Lead Ink Discovery (Sep 2026)

Researchers at UC Berkeley burned their own replica scrolls — had students write on papyrus with traditional lampblack ink, then carbonized them in a furnace replicating Vesuvius temperatures. Discovered: **lead in ancient inks absorbs 25× more X-rays than carbonized papyrus**. Even 25 micrograms/cm² is detectable via handheld X-ray fluorescence scanner.

Practical consequence: can now *screen* all 1,800+ scrolls for lead-containing ink before committing to full CT scanning and virtual unrolling. Massively prioritizes the pipeline.

## What Remains

600+ scrolls still unopened. The library's dominant focus is Epicurean — but PHerc. 1667 suggests non-Epicurean works may be present. Papyrologists note the collection could contain lost works by known authors (Aristotle, Sappho, Ennius) and entirely unknown voices.
