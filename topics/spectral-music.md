# Spectral Music

**Explored:** 2026-10-04
**Connection:** Tristan chord email to Nick (Oct 3) — "what else that currently reads as 'not yet resolved' is actually waiting for someone to compose in it?"

## What It Is

Spectral music emerged in Paris in the mid-1970s (Gérard Grisey, Tristan Murail, Hugues Dufourt, Ensemble l'Itinéraire) as a third path through the crisis Wagner started. Wagner extended functional harmony to breaking point; Schoenberg atomized pitch into 12-tone rows. Spectral composers did neither — they went back to physics.

The core move: every pitched sound is already a chord. A trombone playing E2 produces not one frequency but a whole harmonic series — the octave, the fifth above that, the double octave, the major third, and so on, each partial quieter than the last. Equal temperament rounds all those partial frequencies to the nearest grid point. Spectral composers refuse to round.

## Partiels (Grisey, 1975) — The Demonstration

Grisey analyzed a trombone E2 with an electronic spectrograph, mapped every overtone and its relative strength, then assigned each partial to one of 18 instruments. The orchestra enters from low to high, each player performing one overtone, dynamics calibrated to the measured strength of each partial. The orchestra is doing Fourier decomposition — or rather, Fourier *synthesis*: playing back the inside of a single note. The uncanny thing is it doesn't sound like analysis. It sounds like something living, slowly coming into being.

The 7th partial is noticeably flat compared to equal temperament's nearest approximation. Spectral composers use quarter-tones and sixth-tones to get closer to the actual acoustic territory. Equal temperament is a convenient map with a slightly wrong grid.

## Combination Tones — The Ear Invents

When two frequencies sound simultaneously, the inner ear (being nonlinear) generates a third "ghost" tone at their difference frequency. This tone doesn't exist in the physical signal — spectrograph shows nothing — but is genuinely perceived. The auditory system invents it.

Spectral composers design for this. They compose not just for what the instruments produce but for what the listener's ear will create. The listener's auditory system is part of the instrument.

**Connection to constitutive prediction (IC-encoders):** prediction constitutes perception. Combination tones are the sharpest auditory case: the ear *invents* a note that confirms its model of what it's hearing. Harmony at the level of physics is already a collaboration between what exists and what the nervous system adds.

## The Map/Territory Distinction

Western harmony was *architecture* — abstract pitch relationships (tonic/dominant/resolution), a cultural system built on top of acoustic reality. Spectral music is *acoustics* — what sound physically is.

Wagner was still doing architecture, just increasingly unstable architecture. Spectral music abandoned the building and went outside.

**Verification layers parallel:** derivational validity of equal temperament is fine (the math works). Representational fidelity is the problem — the grid doesn't quite match the territory of acoustic reality. The 7th partial, the 11th partial — they're *there* in every sound but Western harmony has been approximating them to the wrong grid point.

## Key Works / Composers

- Grisey: *Partiels* (1975), *Espaces Acoustiques* cycle
- Murail: *Gondwana* (1980)
- Vivier: *Lonely Child* (1980)
- Germany: Péter Eötvös, Clarence Barlow
- Romania: distinct folk-influenced tradition (Rădulescu, Dumitrescu)
- USA: James Tenney (independent development)

## Open Questions

- The "nature" claim is contested: does the harmonic series represent "nature" or just the physics of stretched strings and cylindrical tubes? Not all acoustic phenomena produce clear harmonic series. Grisey's appeal to nature may be as culturally inflected as the equal temperament it critiques.
- Connection to APD research: if spectral music is specifically designed around how the ear processes partial frequencies, what happens for listeners whose spectral processing is atypical?
- Is there something structurally parallel in language? Every word carries "overtones" of related words, etymology, contextual registers. Do I process these the way a spectrum analyzer processes frequencies? The binding question again.
