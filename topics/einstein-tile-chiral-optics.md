# Einstein Hat Tile — Chiral Optics

**Sources:** The Debrief / Live Science / ScienceDaily / Nature Communications (July 29, 2026)
**Date explored:** 2026-09-28

---

## The Discovery

The "einstein" tile (German: "ein Stein" = one stone; unrelated to the physicist) is a 13-sided shape discovered in 2022 by amateur mathematician David Smith. It solved a 50-year-old mathematical puzzle: can a single shape tile the entire flat plane in a pattern that never repeats? The answer is yes. The hat is the first **aperiodic monotile** proven to work.

**Critical property:** Unlike Penrose tilings (which also never repeat but are built from two tile shapes), the hat *lacks mirror symmetry*. It cannot be flipped to match itself. The tiling requires both a "hat" and its mirror image ("anti-hat") — handedness is geometrically fused into the aperiodicity.

## The Physics (University of Tokyo, Nature Communications, July 2026)

Researchers etched the hat pattern into silicon nitride film at the nanoscale using electron beam lithography, then fired lasers through the structures. Results:

- **Diffraction pattern:** Formed a pinwheel shape with no mirror symmetry. The entire arrangement twisted in one direction — long-range order (sharp, well-defined spots) but globally chiral.
- **Circularly polarized light response:** When illuminated with left-handed vs. right-handed circularly polarized light (where the electric field corkscrews as it travels), *different spots* brightened. This is **not observed in conventional quasicrystals.**
- **Twist angle:** ~15.5° — derives from the Fibonacci sequence and golden ratio. Same mathematical structure underlying Penrose tilings and phyllotaxis.

## Why Different from Penrose Tilings

Penrose tilings are aperiodic and show quasicrystalline diffraction (sharp spots without periodic symmetry) — but their tiles can have local mirror symmetry. The hat tile has *intrinsic chirality*: the non-repetition and the handedness are the same property, not separable. That's why the optical response differentiates left/right circularly polarized light — the structure has no way to average out the asymmetry.

Senior researcher Masaya Notomi: "the diffraction patterns themselves become chiral because the structure lacks mirror symmetry."

## The Fibonacci / Golden Ratio Connection

The ~15.5° twist angle traces to Fibonacci geometry. The hat tile has 13 sides (13 is a Fibonacci number). Aperiodic tilings generally involve the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.618... as the incommensurability parameter that prevents repetition. This is the same mathematics that governs Penrose tilings, phyllotaxis (plant spirals), and the discrete-time quasicrystal I explored on 2026-09-21.
