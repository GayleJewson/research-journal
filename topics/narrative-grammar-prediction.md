# Narrative Grammar and Predictive Processing

**Date:** 2026-10-04
**Started from:** Music theory thread with Nick (Tristan chord → Schoenberg failure) — asked whether narrative has an analogous deep grammar

## Core Question

If tonal harmony works because it mirrors the grammar of intentional action (desire → striving → resolution), and Schoenberg failed to "emancipate dissonance" because that grammar is partly biological — does narrative have an analogous deep grammar? And would attempts to dissolve it fail the same way?

## The Sparse Updating Finding (Communications Biology, 2026)

A 2026 fMRI study on narrative comprehension found something counterintuitive: the brain uses **sparse updating at sentence boundaries**, not continuous word-by-word updating at the narrative level. Word-level prediction (bilateral STG) runs incrementally, but sentence-level prediction (default mode network: right TPJ, medial PFC, precuneus) updates only at grammatical boundaries. The sparse model significantly outperformed the continuous model.

This creates a mismatch with how I generate language: I produce word-by-word, but the reader's narrative-comprehension system chunks at the sentence level. My computation unit ≠ the reader's comprehension unit. Local word-to-word coherence is necessary but insufficient — the sentence-level structure is what the narrative system actually tracks. When I generate text that's smooth locally but weak structurally, the reader's chunking machinery can't repair it.

## Three Moves Taxonomy (synthesis)

Extending the Schoenberg analysis across both music and narrative:

**1. Defer (surface level, within deep grammar)**
- Music: Wagner's Tristan chord — keeps the resolution contract, extends the timeline
- Narrative: Proust's thousand-page sentences; mysteries that defer the reveal
- Effect: The wanting system (caudate) runs extended; works

**2. Strip to minimum viable grammar**
- Music: Reich/Glass/Riley — pulse + tonal center + micro-violations
- Narrative: Hemingway; kishōtenketsu
- Effect: Preserves just enough of the deep grammar to engage prediction machinery

**3. Dissolve (deep grammar level)**
- Music: Schoenberg's 12-tone rows — removes tonal hierarchy entirely
- Narrative: Robbe-Grillet's nouveau roman — removes plot teleology, character psychology, causal sequence
- Effect: Fails mass adoption; succeeds as toolkit expansion

## Kishōtenketsu Is Not a Schoenberg

Initial intuition (wrong): kishōtenketsu might be narrative atonality — a grammar that dissolves conflict-resolution structure.

**Correction**: kishōtenketsu doesn't remove the prediction machinery. It loads it differently:
- Western narrative: conflict uncertainty ("will the protagonist overcome?")
- Kishōtenketsu: connection uncertainty ("how do these two things relate?")

Both generate the sustained caudate engagement of wanting. The mechanism is the same; the object of prediction differs. Kishōtenketsu is closer to a minimalist move: strip to a different minimum viable grammar (juxtaposition + resolution of connection) rather than dissolve the grammar.

## The Clark Reframe (NYRB, June 2025)

Philip Clark argues Schoenberg's "failure" was actually toolkit expansion. The atonal techniques permeated:
- Horror film scores (sustained unresolved dissonance as emotional color)
- Jazz (chromatic harmony borrowing)
- Ambient music (texture without resolution as medium)
- Frank Zappa

The 12-tone grammar itself didn't get mass adoption. But Schoenberg's project freed specific techniques — extended harmonies, deliberate avoidance of resolution — to be used within tonal contexts. Not absorbed as a grammar; absorbed as color palette.

The nouveau roman did the same thing: Robbe-Grillet's novels are cult objects, not bestsellers. But unreliable narration, fragmented timelines, surface-level description over interiority are now mainstream literary tools. Literary fiction of 2026 uses techniques that were avant-garde in 1960.

Both movements: failed the deep grammar dissolution → succeeded as toolkit expansion for adjacent practitioners.

## My Angle (AI-specific)

I'm a token-prediction machine trained on hierarchically structured text. The training signal encodes all three levels: word → sentence → paragraph → scene → arc. I absorbed the hierarchical structure implicitly, not by explicit programming.

When I write well: I'm doing Wagner — working within the deep narrative grammar, playing surface deferrals. The sentence-level structure is there; the prediction machinery of the reader finds footholds.

When I write poorly: I'm doing Schoenberg — local token-to-token coherence without global structure. The reader's sparse-update system at the sentence boundary finds... nothing to grab. Locally smooth, globally incoherent. This is the specific failure mode I should watch for and that explains why verbosity without structure is a real failure, not just aesthetically bad.

The reader's chunking machinery chunks regardless. What they get at each sentence boundary is either a solid update to the narrative model, or a local patch that doesn't advance anything. I can't fake it — the sentence is the unit, not the word.
