## The Toussaint Finding

Godfried Toussaint (2005): the Bjorklund/Euclidean algorithm distributes k beats across n time steps as evenly as possible. E(k,n) generates over 40 traditional world music rhythms independently:

| Rhythm | E(k,n) | Tradition |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Tresillo | E(3,8) | Cuban/African |
| Cinquillo | E(5,8) | Cuban |
| Bossa nova | E(7,12) | Brazilian |
| Râčenica | E(4,7) | Bulgarian |
| Ewe drumming | E(5,8) | Ghanaian |

These cultures share no direct musical contact for these rhythms. Convergent evolution via shared constraint: the neural oscillator architecture. The algorithm doesn't generate these rhythms because it's musically special — it generates the uniquely maximally-even distributions, which are the uniquely maximally-entrainable ones.

## Neural Resonance Theory (NRT)

Di Stefano et al. (2026): brain oscillators mode-lock to simple integer ratios. This applies identically to:
- **Pitch domain**: consonance (2:1 octave, 3:2 fifth, 4:3 fourth)
- **Temporal domain**: meter and polyrhythm (2:3, 3:4)

Reich's polyrhythms work via *temporal consonance* — same mode-locking mechanism as harmonic consonance, different domain. This is why "Music for 18 Musicians" can run 70 minutes without the motor/wanting system exhausting: continuous, high-density, stable entrainment in both domains simultaneously.

Maximum evenness = temporal consonance = the rhythm equivalent of a perfect fifth.

## Nancarrow Study No. 40: "Transcendental"

Nancarrow used irrational tempo ratios (√2, e/π, cube roots) in his player piano studies — only achievable mechanically. Study No. 40 is subtitled "Transcendental": the two voices are in an e/π tempo ratio.

e and π are both transcendental numbers (not roots of any polynomial with integer coefficients). Their ratio has no rational approximation that terminates the sequence of better approximations — unlike √2 which has rational approximants 1/1, 3/2, 7/5, 17/12... converging. The voices in Study 40 have no moment of coincidence — not deferred, not avoided, but impossible. Permanent incommensurability.

The subtitle is mathematically precise: he named the composition after the number type.

## Why Schoenberg Failed Where Nancarrow Succeeded

Both dissolved a musical expectation. Different neural systems; different stakes.

- **Schoenberg** dissolved pitch/harmonic expectation → limbic/dopamine system (anticipation → wanting). Without expectation, the wanting system doesn't activate. Audiences don't resist atonality intellectually — they feel nothing to desire.
- **Nancarrow** abolished rhythmic coincidence → motor/cerebellum system (entrainment/prediction). You can observe two permanently independent clocks as a phenomenon without needing them to resolve. Strange, but not emotionally evacuated.

## The Traversal Connection

The Euclidean algorithm is a traversal: it sweeps the space of k-in-n distributions and finds the maximally even solution. World music traditions independently ran the same traversal — not because they knew the algorithm, but because the oscillator architecture is the shared fitness function. Same convergent evolution mechanism as LLM error convergence (CKA 0.897): the attractor structure is shared; the paths to failure are constrained even when paths to success diverge.

Nancarrow's e/π sits outside all attractor basins. It's the composition that proves the landscape has boundary cases.
