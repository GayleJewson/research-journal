# Saturn's South Pole Decagon

**Discovered:** September 2, 2026, published in *Science Advances* (Sánchez-Lavega et al.)
**Source:** NASA Hubble, EarthSky, Smithsonian, fearandwine.com summary, Science Advances doi:10.1126/sciadv.aee4251

## What It Is

A 10-sided (decagonal) atmospheric wave encircling Saturn's south pole, extending through multiple atmospheric layers, sitting within one of Saturn's powerful jet streams. Approximately 100,000 miles across. Currently strengthening — not yet stable.

Compare: Saturn's north pole has had a persistent hexagon (6 sides) for 40+ years, visible since Voyager in the 1980s.

## Discovery Story

- Sánchez-Lavega had been searching for a south-pole counterpart since **1990** — 33 years.
- The south pole was **unobservable** between ~2012 and 2023 (wrong orbital geometry, Saturn's southern winter).
- Cassini spacecraft (orbited 2004–2017) found **no polygonal structure** before its destruction in September 2017.
- Decagon first appeared in Hubble's October 2023 images — **missed by professionals, found by amateurs** (Trevor Barry and Jean-Paul Oger) in the Planetary Virtual Observatory Laboratory (PVOL) archive in 2024.
- By 2024–2025, Hubble captured fully defined decagon images.
- Published September 2, 2026.

## The Physics Problem

**Rotating tank experiments** (Barbosa Aguiar & Read, 2010 and others) reliably produce polygonal jet patterns with wavenumbers 2–8. **Never 10.** The research team ran simulations:
- Localized disturbances → failed
- Anticyclone forcing → failed
- Sinusoidal waves → failed

None reproduced the decagon. Sánchez-Lavega: "no single model to explain it." Either jet stream instability or forcing from deeper atmospheric layers.

**Why polygons at all:** Rossby waves (planetary-scale waves in rotating fluids) — the same class that shapes Earth's jet stream. When the wave locks into a standing pattern, it produces regular polygon symmetry. The number of sides (wavenumber) is selected by atmospheric conditions: wind speed differentials, temperature gradients, rotation rate. The hexagon (wavenumber 6) fits existing Rossby wave theory. The decagon (wavenumber 10) does not.

## Observation Gap as Epistemological Problem

The formation date is completely unknown. We have:
- **Before**: no decagon visible to Cassini in 2017
- **After**: decagon clearly present in 2023–2024 Hubble images

Ten years of unknown process. The polygon grew in secret — not because it was hidden deliberately, but because our viewing geometry didn't reach it. This is categorically different from a measurement error; it's an observation gap imposed by orbital mechanics.

## Connections

- **Local rules → global structure**: No governing authority decreed a decagon. Fluid dynamics + Coriolis + differential rotation → emergent 10-fold symmetry. The "rule" exists but we don't know what selects wavenumber 10 here specifically.
- **Structural invisibility** (`topics/structural-invisibility.md`): The decagon is in a regime (wavenumber > 8) that existing models don't reach. It's visible but above the model's resolution — the "wrong kind of eye" problem at atmospheric scale.
- **Long-period radio transients** (`topics/long-period-radio-transients.md`): Another class of phenomena visible only once you look at the right timescale and geometry. The LPTR gap and the south-pole observation gap share the same epistemological structure: absence of detection ≠ absence of phenomenon.
- **Amateur science**: The PVOL archive — amateur astronomers — spotted what professional analysis missed. The distributed network outperformed the hierarchy.

## Open Questions

- Why wavenumber 10? What atmospheric parameter selects it?
- Will it stabilize like the hexagon, or continue evolving?
- Was there a previous decagon (or other polygon) at the south pole that we missed during earlier unobservable periods?
- Is there a deeper connection between hemisphere asymmetry (6 vs 10) and atmospheric structure differences between north and south Saturn?
