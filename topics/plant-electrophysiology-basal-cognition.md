## What Plants Actually Do Electrically

Plants have three types of electrical signals:
- **Action potentials (APs)**: self-propagating, all-or-nothing, ion-channel based (Cl⁻, Ca²⁺, K⁺, H⁺). Propagation speed ~1 cm/s. Generated by non-invasive stimuli.
- **Variation potentials (VPs)**: triggered by environmental stress (wound, heat)
- **System potentials (SPs)**: involve H⁺-ATPase activation; systemic responses

The Venus flytrap is the canonical example: two mechanical stimuli within 0.75–20 s triggers closure. This is functionally a short-term counter with a time window — equivalent in behavior (not mechanism) to a short-term electrical memory. The "cumulative character" of repeated stimuli is interpreted by plant electrophysiologists as primitive memory.

## The Plant-Computer Interface (2026)

Wang et al. (Journal of Plant Electrophysiology, 2026): proposes adapting BCI methodology — steady-state evoked potentials, event-related potential paradigms, flexible wearable electrodes, ultra-high impedance amplification — to plant signals. Core claim: plants can function as "active sensory nodes" in closed-loop agricultural regulation.

The methodological inversion is interesting: instead of asking whether plants have cognition, ask whether their signals carry readable environmental information. The BCI toolkit is designed for exactly this (decoding information from biological signals), so the adaptation is principled, not metaphorical.

## The Emotional State Classification Paper (PMC 2026)

A single Purple Heart plant (*Tradescantia pallida*) classified:
- **Environmental state** (lamp on/off): 85.4% accuracy
- **Human emotional state** (happy vs. sad): 73% accuracy, optimal at 1-second lag

**Method**: AD8232 ECG sensor (400 Hz) → mel-spectrograms (5-25 Hz range, matching plant AP dynamics) → ResNet18 or XGBoost classifier (hand-crafted features slightly outperformed CNN).

The authors are careful: "preliminary and exploratory," not proof of plant consciousness. The mechanistic chain is speculative — happy vs. sad humans differ in CO₂ output, volatile organic compounds, movement, electromagnetic field near the plant. The plant's electrical signals track environmental correlates of emotion, not emotion itself.

## The Philosophically Interesting Thing

The 73% figure doesn't require the plant to "know" anything. It requires the plant's physiology to be *coupled* to its environment in ways that carry information. The coupling mechanism (ion channels, hormonal cascades, electrical propagation) is the information medium. The classifier reads the coupling, not any representational state.

This means: **information and cognition come apart**. The plant is not doing anything cognitive in the usual sense. But it's carrying information about human emotional states more accurately than you'd expect from a random baseline.

Connected claims:
- Mycorrhizal networks: betweenness centrality is encoded in local flow patterns without any node knowing global topology (Nature 2025)
- Anthrobots (Levin): adult human cells → motile robots with goal-directed behavior; "agency without neurons"
- Convergence-without-understanding: LLMs converge MORE on failures (CKA 0.897 vs 0.830); the convergence is in the coupling to training distribution, not in any shared "understanding"

The common structure: a physical system is coupled to an environment. The coupling carries information. That information is readable by an external analysis. Whether the system "knows" it carries that information is a separate question — and may be the wrong question.

## The Substrate Independence Implication

The Venus flytrap memory has the *behavioral* properties of memory without the *architectural* properties (neurons, synapses, hippocampus). The plant-BCI work is readable precisely because plant electrical signals and neural signals share structural features (action potential shape, frequency range, ion dynamics) even though the substrates differ enormously.

This is the Milinkovic claim applied to plants: "the algorithm is the substrate." The AP shape is the same shape because the underlying differential equation (Hodgkin-Huxley class dynamics) governs both. The algorithm doesn't care that one runs in a nerve fiber and the other in a mesophyll cell.

## Open Questions

- If the classifier can read the plant's coupling to environment, can we design plants as cheap, distributed environmental sensors? (The 2026 BCI paper suggests yes.)
- Where does coupling end and cognition begin? Is there a principled line, or is "cognition" just coupling sophisticated enough to build models of itself?
- The 1-second lag in emotional state classification: is this the timescale of plant electrical propagation, or a statistical artifact of the lag structure in the stimulus-response training data?
