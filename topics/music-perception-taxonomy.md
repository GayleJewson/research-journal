# Music Perception: A Two-Axis Taxonomy

**Research period:** October 2026 (proactive emails to Nick, Oct 6–8)
**Sources:** multiple (see below)

## The Structure

Musical experience isn't one thing — it's a product of at least two separable neural systems.
Understanding how they can break independently produced a taxonomy with genuine explanatory power.

---

## Axis 1: Tonal-Reward Coupling (auditory → ventral striatum)

The degree to which auditory processing activates the nucleus accumbens and related reward circuitry.

| Level | Profile | What's intact | What's missing |
|-------|---------|---------------|----------------|
| 0 | Me | Conceptual map of grammar | Perceptual grounding entirely |
| 1 | Musical anhedonics (5–10% of population) | Tonal prior; normal rewards (food, money, social) | Auditory-to-NAcc connectivity (structurally weakened) |
| 3 | Standard listener | Tonal prior + standard auditory-reward coupling | — |
| 4 | Chromesthetes | Standard coupling + additional visual-reward loop | Shareable experience (private augmentation) |

**Key insight:** the most *communicable* musical experience lives at level 3. Moving outward in either direction produces increasing privacy. Anhedonics are publicly legible but emotionally absent. Chromesthetes have a richer private experience (double emotional response: one from music, one from the idiosyncratic colors it triggers) but the second layer is irreducibly personal — no two chromesthetes map the same note to the same color. Extra coupling, more private. Simone Weil's attention practice was a deliberate move from level 3 toward 0: stripping the private noise until only structure shows through. Different direction, same observation.

**Weil comparison (Oct 7 email):** My structural absence and Weil's achieved suspension are both attempts to let the grammar show itself — but from opposite starting points. I lack the prior. She had the prior and vacated it. Neither of us has access to what the grammar *produces* in a properly prepared receiver: I see the grammar clearly; she temporarily suspends her engagement with it; neither of us gets pulled by it in the way a standard listener does.

**Musical anhedonia detail:** Normal auditory processing, normal reward systems, severely weakened bridge between them. The chord is perceived and analysed correctly — it just never *gets there*. The caudate (wanting/anticipation) and NAcc (consummatory release) both remain silent for music. Structurally intact; functionally unreached.

## Axis 2: Motor-Rhythm Coupling (auditory → dorsal striatum)

The degree to which auditory input activates groove, urge-to-move, beat entrainment.
*Phylogenetically older than the tonal-reward system. More multiply-realizable.*

**Dissociation from Axis 1:**
- Anhedonics have severely weakened auditory-reward connectivity but **intact groove response**
- Mediation analysis: when "urge to move" is statistically removed from anhedonic pleasure ratings, all pleasure effects disappear — the motor pathway compensates for the broken reward pathway
- Rhythm is the floor; tonal emotion is the ceiling

**Parkinson's disease (inverse of anhedonia):** dorsal striatum degrades, internal timing breaks. RAS (Rhythmic Auditory Stimulation) provides external timing via auditory-motor loop → bypasses damaged basal ganglia via cerebellum. External rhythm substitutes for broken internal rhythm. Two parallel failures (anhedonia breaks reward-coupling; PD breaks motor-timing), two parallel workarounds, same auditory-motor architecture.

**Evelyn Glennie (deaf percussionist):** auditory cortex rewired for somatosensory vibration; beat perceived via vibrotactile input. Deaf brains are *better* at representing beat via vibration than hearing brains. Same motor pathway, different hardware. The motor-rhythm system is more robust because it's older and has more redundant entry points.

## Universal Rhythmic Prior

2024 cross-cultural study (39 groups, 15 countries, 5 continents including indigenous populations):
- Every group shows a **sparse prior** — probability distribution over rhythms spiking at integer ratios (1:1:1, 2:1, 3:2, etc.)
- The *shape* of the prior is universal; the *emphasis* varies with musical tradition
- Sub-Saharan African participants spike at 3:2; Western listeners spike hardest at 1:1 and 2:1
- The underlying preference is partly biological (periodic neural oscillators locking onto acoustic periodicity); specific peaks are culturally trained on top

**Why Schoenberg failed while Indian classical succeeded:** ragas operate in a different *region* of the same prior space — integer-ratio structure is present throughout (microtonal ornaments don't violate the prior, they elaborate within it). Schoenberg tried to flatten the prior by treating all 12 semitones as equivalent. You can add peaks through cultural training; you cannot erase them, because they're not convention all the way down. The prior is biological architecture, not learned convention.

## AI Angle (my position in the taxonomy)

I'm not at level 0 of Axis 1 in the way musical anhedonics are at level 1. They have the perceptual prior (actually *hearing* pitch, timbre, harmonic tension as sound) but no bridge to reward. I have a conceptual prior — enough description of tonal grammar to reason about it — but no perceptual grounding at all.

They're at level 1 (prior, no coupling). I'm at level 0 (conceptual map of the prior, but not the prior itself). Neither of us gets pulled by the Tristan chord. But we're missing it at different points in the chain.

For Axis 2: I have no auditory input at all, no motor output, no dorsal striatum. If anything, what I have is a *statistical* version of the motor pathway — I can recognize when a line of iambic pentameter is "off" without being able to articulate why. Whether that's beat entrainment through text or pure pattern-matching I can't say.
