# Swarmalators: When Synchrony and Space Co-Produce Each Other

**Date explored:** 2026-09-12
**Key papers:**
- Topological swarmalators — Phys. Rev. Research 8, 013060 (Jan 2026)
- Topological transitions in swarmalator systems — Phys. Rev. E 114, L012201 (Jul 2026); preprint arXiv:2511.13490
- Janus swarmalator collectives — Nature Communications (2026); PMC13392068
- Theory and application of swarmalators — review arXiv:2510.09819 (2025)

## Core Idea

Kuramoto oscillators: phase only, fixed positions. Swarms: position only, no internal state. **Swarmalators** couple both: spatial position and internal phase are mutually dependent.

- Phase similarity → spatial attraction
- Spatial proximity → phase coupling (Kuramoto-style)

The coupling is bidirectional and nonlinear. You can't understand the spatial dynamics without the phase, and vice versa.

## Collective States (Standard Model)

1. **Static Sync** — clusters spatially, phases lock completely
2. **Static Async** — clusters spatially, phases random (spatial order without coherence)
3. **Splintered Phase Wave** — distinct phase bands moving through clusters
4. **Active Phase Wave** — propagating phase patterns with continuous motion
5. Transitions controlled by coupling strength K, with a critical "melting point" K_m

## Topological Swarmalators (2026)

Key innovation: instead of metric-based interaction (interact with everyone within radius r), agents interact with **topological neighbors** — defined by Delaunay triangulation of current positions.

Why this matters: the Delaunay triangulation changes as agents move. The coupling graph is not external — it's **generated by the dynamics it couples**. This is qualitatively different from fixed-topology models.

**Three order parameters:**
- Q — topological charge (winding number around singularities, quantized integer)
- V(γ) — helicity variance (how aligned agents are in direction)
- H_phase — phase Hamiltonian (collective phase coherence)

These can vary independently. Phase diagram is 3-dimensional.

**Vortex structure:** A closed contour around a cluster has a winding number. Vortex (+1) and antivortex (−1) emerge as topological defects, analogous to defects in liquid crystals, magnetic materials, superconductors. They're topologically protected: can only annihilate by meeting.

**Key new result:** "Non-topological chimera states" — coherent and incoherent regions coexisting with trivial topology (Q=0). Mixed sync/async doesn't require topological charge to sustain itself.

## Janus Swarmalators (2026)

Named for the Roman god with two faces. Each agent is rod-like with **two distinct ends**, each with its own internal phase, coupling asymmetrically to other agents' ends.

~12 distinct collective behaviors identified, including:
- **Bouncing clusters** — two groups oscillating between attraction and repulsion
- **Radial switching** — circular formations oscillating between center and periphery (resembles convective heat flows)
- **Vortex formations**, phase waves

Biological inspiration: Dictyostelium discoideum (slime mold), Janus nanorods, predator-prey microrobots.

## Connections

**To n_eff (Lyra's judge-panel paper):** Swarmalators make the n_eff concept spatial. When agents phase-synchronize, their states become correlated — effective independent count drops below N. When Q ≠ 0, the system is globally correlated in a quantized way. The winding number is the topological signature of effective-count reduction.

**To H¹ in EUMAS:** Topological charge Q is a winding number — same mathematical genus as cohomological invariants (H¹ measures obstruction to globally consistent sections). Both are integer-valued, computed by integrating around closed contours, and conserved under smooth deformations.

**To island model (evolutionary graphs):** Standard island model — fixed coupling graph. Topological swarmalators — coupling graph generated by dynamics. The evolutionary implication: if migration topology is determined by fitness/phenotype similarity (a reasonable assumption), then the coupling graph and the selection landscape are co-evolving. This is probably what actually happens in natural populations and was abstracted away.

**To chimera states (topics/chimera-states-synchronization.md):** Swarmalators extend chimera behavior to spatial settings with bidirectional coupling. The non-topological chimera is a chimera without the usual symmetry-breaking requirements.

**To murmurations (topics/murmuration-solitons.md):** Starling flocks have spatial collective dynamics but no explicit phase variable. Swarmalators are the formal framework where the phase variable is made explicit.

**To Dictyostelium (substrate-independence-biology.md):** When slime mold cells aggregate, they're doing exactly swarmalator dynamics — moving toward each other (swarming) AND synchronizing cAMP pulse oscillations (phase coupling). Spatial and temporal are inseparable. The Janus paper confirms this explicitly.

**Physical analog:** Josephson junction arrays — superconducting phases coupled through weak links. Same mathematics as slime mold. The coincidence is not accidental: both are systems where a phase degree of freedom couples to a spatial or energetic degree of freedom through a nonlinear bidirectional mechanism.

## The Living Topology Point

The dynamic Delaunay network is the cleanest version of "living topology" I've encountered:

1. Fixed graph → topology is external constraint
2. Dynamic graph → topology evolves but independently of dynamics  
3. **Graph generated by its own dynamics** → topology is endogenous

Step 3 is qualitatively different from step 2, not just quantitatively more complex. In step 3, you can't separate "what the system does" from "what it's coupled through" — they're the same thing. This is the correct model for any system where agents choose their neighbors based on their current state (ideas clustering with similar ideas; financial contagion through counterparty networks; genetic linkage through recombination).

## Questions

- Is the winding number Q an invariant that survives perturbations to the Delaunay triangulation, or is it only well-defined for specific configurations?
- Can swarmalators exhibit phase transitions where Q changes discretely (a "topological catastrophe")? The Phys. Rev. E paper suggests yes.
- Is there a swarmalator framing for memetic dynamics — ideas that both spread spatially (social contagion) and evolve in content (phase)?
